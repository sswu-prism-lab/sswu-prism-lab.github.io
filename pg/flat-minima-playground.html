Flat minima, thermal noise, and free energy

Things to try
  1. Trapped: at the start (low T) the ball is stuck in the narrow well.
  2. Thermal escape: raise T to cross the barrier; over time the ball spends longer in the wide well (mass bars below).
  3. Generalization: move s to see how the gap differs between the narrow and wide wells.
  4. SAM objective: turn on SAM to see how shallow the narrow well becomes under the sharpness-aware loss.

Wide well = low curvature

When the test distribution is offset by s (covariate shift), the narrow well climbs its steep walls and loss spikes, while the wide well barely changes. So low-curvature κ minima have a smaller generalization gap.

Temperature = Langevin noise

SGD ≈ gradient descent + thermal noise, with stationary distribution p ∝ e^(−L/T). This minimizes the free energy F = ⟨E⟩ − T·S, favoring flat minima whose entropy S (basin volume) is large.

SAM: worst-neighbor loss

It minimizes the worst-case loss within a ρ-ball, W(θ)=max L(θ+δ). The narrow well is steep right next door so W spikes and it is avoided; the wide well barely moves and is selected.
